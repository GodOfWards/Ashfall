# Ashfall — Canon

**Private.** Nothing here is ever stated by the game. Room, item and event
text implies a piece of it at most, per the Project Guide, Part 1. Who reads
this file, and when, is set in `CLAUDE.md` ("The canon is private").

## What this file is

The world as the designers know it: what the collapse was, when things
happened, and the real places the town is drawn from. It exists so that
power, water, spoilage, threats and every dated line of text agree with each
other.

It records only what Tom has decided. Anything still open is listed as open
rather than guessed. Research is kept apart from decisions, in `reference/`: a
fact there is true of the real world, not of the game, until a decision here
adopts it.

## Settled

- **The town is Lima,** in the Partido de Zárate, Buenos Aires province,
  Argentina: a small grid town with the Atucha nuclear complex about 8 km to
  its north and the city of Zárate about 16 km away (#237). The world is as real and accurate as it can be
  made: real names, facts checked against a source (`CLAUDE.md`, "The world
  is real"). Chosen 2026-09-26, replacing Henderson, Kentucky. The game's
  world, written for an invented American town, is being rebuilt on it
  (#237).
- **The collapse comes in February 2025** (Tom, 2026-09-26). The world is
  as it stood then. Each subject uses the latest data that describes it as of
  February 2025. Where nothing newer exists, the latest earlier source stands:
  for population and households that's the 2022 national census (*Censo
  Nacional de Población, Hogares y Viviendas*, 18 May 2022). A source written
  later still counts if it shows what was there by February 2025. **Day zero
  is the day the airborne wave broke in Lima** (below); its exact date is
  open.
- **The collapse is a zombie apocalypse** (Tom, 2026-09-27). The game never
  says so; the threat itself is #12. What it was, and how it played out in
  Lima, is under "The collapse" below.
- **Atucha is in the game, but only what someone on the road would see**
  (Tom, 2026-09-27; #285). The fence, the gatehouse, the car parks, the
  contractors' yard for Atucha I's overhaul and the substation are reachable.
  The reactor buildings stay locked. Atucha I's overhaul (about 2,000 jobs)
  is texture in Lima itself: rented rooms, work trucks, site canteens, PPE.
- **No radiation mechanic,** unless the research shows that lack of
  maintenance really makes radioactive material a hazard (Tom, 2026-09-27;
  #290).
- **The player is from Lima** (Tom, 2026-09-27; #292). They know its streets
  and public landmarks as a local does, but not what's inside or what's left.
  **Their home is a row house in the Barrio Complejo Nuclear Atucha** (Tom,
  2026-09-27; #284), the terraced strip on the barrio's western side, north
  of the railway. Why they are still there is under "The player" below.
- **The game is in English; places keep their Spanish names** (`CLAUDE.md`,
  "Writing game text").
- **The wider setting is a string of towns with a city nearby:** Lima first,
  then others along the same corridor (Zárate, Baradero, San Pedro are the
  candidates in `reference/Lima.md`). Which ones, and when, is open.
- **A run starts a few days after day zero, at most** (#218). The exact
  figure is a constant in `ashfall.html` and a future world-generation
  option (#149); it is not restated here.
- **Grid power fails on its own day after the collapse, and running water
  fails with it** (#260). Why, and how, is under "The grid, water and gas"
  below.

## The collapse

Decided by Tom in the lore planning session of 2026-09-29/30 (#329). Figures
marked as first guesses are retunable; the reference files named hold the
real-world facts behind each part.

### The virus

- **Two strains, one end.** An **airborne strain** infected a high
  percentage of people; everyone it kills rises, and **in the dead body the
  virus mutates into the bite strain**, which is what the risen carry. The
  dead don't breathe, so the virus goes on by biting — implied, never
  stated.
- **The airborne strain is subtle.** First guesses: about **5–10 days**
  without symptoms but already infectious; **2–3 days** of mild, cold-like
  symptoms; about **a day** of fever and confusion; death, then rising
  within minutes.
- **The bite strain** passes through **bites and bodily fluids**, and **a
  bite is a death sentence.** First guesses: symptoms after hours to about
  two days, sooner the nearer the bite is to the head (as with rabies);
  about a day of fever and confusion; death; rising within minutes. The
  sick are infectious through their fluids, so carers catch it.
- **The dying are not aggressive,** under either strain. Only the risen
  dead are.
- **The airborne strain can be protected against:** catching it isn't
  inevitable (sealed, filtered rooms and fitted respirators are the
  real-world analogues, `reference/Grid_AR.md`).
- **Some people are resistant to the airborne strain.** No one resists the
  bite strain, so most of the resistant were bitten later.
- **Humans only.** No animal carries it or is affected.
- **It began abroad** and surfaced in several cities at once, carried by air
  travellers still incubating. **It arrived as COVID-19 had:** news from
  abroad, first cases, spread before anyone knew. **COVID-19 happened in
  this world too**, so in February 2025 everyone remembered it, and the
  authorities had its playbook (`reference/Outbreak_Response.md`).

### The dead

- **They bite and try to feed,** but one alone usually isn't strong enough
  to hold a victim down; several can. So most victims got away, bitten.
- **They don't eat each other, and don't need to eat:** the body slowly
  consumes itself, which is why they are **walkers**, not runners.
- **Senses:** hearing first, sight at short range, poor in the dark. **No
  shared sense**: only the one that perceives the player goes for them,
  though others can be drawn by its noise and movement, as by any sound.
- **They decay slowly** and wear out over months.
- **What stops them:** enough head trauma, or enough damage to the body;
  the head takes far less.

### How Lima emptied

- **Day zero is the day the airborne wave broke in Lima**: the first wave
  of deaths and risings in town, in **mid-to-late February 2025**. The exact
  date needn't be fixed yet. It has to fit the summer: two-week holidays
  fall between the second half of January and the first half of February,
  the 2025 school year began on 5 March, and secondary schools were open
  for students owing subjects on 14–27 February (`reference/Calendar_AR.md`).
- **Before it:** about one to two weeks of COVID-like news. **Most people
  were back from their holidays and went to work as normal,** with
  precautions at first, **then a lockdown**, as in 2020.
- **Lima was sealed** during the lockdown, as real towns in the province
  were in 2020: a checkpoint and earth mounds on its accesses.
- **Families were shut in together by the lockdown**, so most of Lima's dead
  died, and rose, **at home**.
- **The Atucha I overhaul's contractors** (about 2,000), as a first guess:
  about half were back in Lima from their holidays when it closed, locked
  down in rented rooms; the other half went straight home and never came
  back.

### The player

- **Resistant to the airborne strain**, not bitten, and **not the only one
  who was resistant**; a bite would kill them like anyone else.
- **Their family was away** when it came, and the sealed town kept them from
  coming back. The house is still a family's house. Who the family is, and
  where they were, is open. Why the player stayed behind isn't fixed here:
  their occupation is chosen at character creation (#9).

### What was left in the shops

- **Panic buying during the news** took what it took in March 2020: hand
  sanitiser, cleaning products, masks, and stockpiled staples (flour, pasta,
  rice, yerba, toilet paper). **Pharmacies lost their cold and fever
  medicine first**: the airborne strain's mild stage looks like a summer
  cold.
- **Under the lockdown only essential shops stayed open** — neighbourhood
  shops and supermarkets, pharmacies, hardware stores, garrafa supply, as
  under DNU 297/2020 — and every other shop has its shutters down and its
  stock untouched.
- **On day zero the open shops stopped mid-day.** Where a shop has its home
  behind it, the owner often died and rose there.
- **Little looting:** only a few days, very few survivors, and the dead
  don't loot. At most the odd broken shopfront.
- **So** most shops are well stocked but for what panic buying took;
  hygiene, medicine and staples are scarce, tinned and bottled goods
  plentiful, perishables spoiling (#325).

### The grid, water and gas

- **Grid operators sealed themselves in** during the news, before the
  airborne strain reached them, as they had been sequestered in 2020. The
  grid's centre held. But repair crews can't be sealed, so **every fault out
  on the lines stayed unrepaired**, and the grid lost pieces as it went
  (`reference/Grid_AR.md`).
- **On day 21 it failed** (the game's `POWER_FAILS_DAY`; the lore makes the
  day plausible, not exact): *weeks of unrepaired losses left it weakened
  and run hard, with its automatic protections set for a grid that no
  longer existed and nothing left to shed. An ordinary fault cascaded
  through the whole system in seconds, as it had on 16 June 2019, and there
  was no one to restart it.* The real 2019 sequence is in `Grid_AR.md`.
- **For Lima it is one event**: the whole town at once. At night the glow of
  Zárate and Campana on the horizon goes out with it.
- **A false recovery:** hours later, the sealed crews' attempt to restart
  brings the power back briefly, then it fails for good, as Yacyretá's
  machines tripped while taking load in 2019 (#347).
- **Distant islands stay lit for a while**, around the big dams, as the
  north-east did in 2019. Never near Lima.
- **Running water fails with the grid** (#260).
- **Mains gas fades over the days after the grid.** No one kept the gas
  chain running, so the treatment plants stopped with the grid; the
  street network's regulators are mechanical, so gas flows while the
  pipelines hold pressure against what little demand is left, and a
  cooker's flame shrinks before it goes out. How many days is a design
  figure for #342 (`reference/Gas_AR.md`).

## Open

- **Day zero's exact date** in mid-to-late February 2025.
- **What happens to Atucha after the collapse** (#290, #285): Atucha II
  running and Atucha I mid-overhaul in February 2025, and the grid failing
  on day 21. Whether its crew sealed themselves in as the grid operators
  did, and so whether its lights stay on the horizon after the grid fails
  (Atucha II rode through 2019 on its own auxiliaries), is the question.
  The game never states it.
- **What share of people the airborne strain infected.** It sets how many
  dead are in Lima (#12).
- **How the airborne strain was kept out** by those who did (sealed rooms,
  respirators, which of them worked).
- **The player's family:** who they are, and where they were.
- **Fictional brands and trading names** (Tom, 2026-09-27): no real brand or
  private business name appears in the game (`CLAUDE.md`, "The world is
  real"). Inventing fictional ones is lore, for later.
- **How old each building is.** Its wiring era decides its panel size, where
  it has residual-current protection, and whether its water heater is gas
  or electric (#237, #251).
