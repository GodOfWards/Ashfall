# Ashfall — Canon

**Private.** Nothing here is ever stated by the game. Room, item and event
text implies a piece of it at most, per the Project Guide, Part 1. Who reads
this file, and when, is set in `CLAUDE.md` ("The canon is private").

## What this file is

The world as the designers know it: what the collapse was, when things
happened, and the real places the town is drawn from. It exists so that
power, water, spoilage, threats and every dated line of text agree with each
other.

It records only what Tom has decided. Anything still open is listed as open
rather than guessed, and research is kept apart from decisions: a fact under
**Reference** is true of the real world, not of the game, until a decision
adopts it.

## Settled

- **The town is modelled on Henderson, Kentucky**, on the Ohio River across
  from Evansville, Indiana. How literally the in-game map follows the real
  town is not yet decided.
- **A run starts a few days after the collapse, at most** (#218). The exact
  figure is a constant in `ashfall.html` and a future world-generation
  option (#149); it is not restated here.

## Open

- **What day zero is.** The day it began everywhere, or the day this town
  emptied.
- **Why the player is still in town** a few days after (#137).
- **Why grid power fails when it does** (#220). Proposed, not decided: an
  accumulation of unrepaired faults, area by area, rather than one event.
- **Real names or stand-ins** for utilities and places.

## Reference — Henderson's real infrastructure

Researched September 2026. Real-world facts, not canon until adopted.

**Electricity.**
- Henderson Municipal Power & Light (HMP&L) is the city's utility. It owns
  transmission and distribution but no generation: Station One, its coal
  plant on Water Street by the river, closed in 2008; Station Two, at the
  Sebree site to the south, was retired in 2019 and demolished in 2023.
- HMP&L buys through MISO, the regional grid operator, and has a 20-year
  contract for the 50 MW Henderson County Solar farm.
- HMP&L substations reported: just east of the Borax Drive / US 41A-60
  intersection; just west of the Atkinson Street intersection; one serving
  the north end of town and Highway 60 East.
- The rural county is served by Kenergy, a co-op headquartered in
  Henderson, supplied by Big Rivers Electric. Big Rivers' Green Station at
  Sebree now burns natural gas (500 MW); the Reid extra-high-voltage
  substation sits on the Webster/Henderson county line. Century Aluminum's
  Sebree smelter draws about 368 MW.
- Across the river in Indiana: the A.B. Brown, F.B. Culley and Alcoa Warrick
  plants.
- Grid-tied solar inverters shut down when the grid goes dead, so a solar
  farm cannot power a town on its own.

**Water.**
- Henderson Water Utility's North Water Treatment Plant draws from the Ohio
  River. The system has 12 storage tanks holding 14.6 million gallons and
  4 pumping stations. The Atkinson Park reservoir (4.5 million gallons) has
  been in use since 1946.

**Gas and crossings.**
- Texas Gas Transmission runs pipelines through Henderson County. The city's
  gas distributor was not confirmed.
- The US 41 Twin Bridges cross the Ohio to Evansville (1932 and 1966).
