# How a real outbreak was handled — COVID-19, 2020–2021

Real-world facts, not canon until a decision adopts them (`../Ashfall_Canon.md`
records what's decided). See `README.md` for the status labels. Gathered for
the lore session on how the collapse played out in Lima (#329), at Tom's
request (2026-09-29): what a government, a town and a health system actually
did the last time, and where they broke, as the floor for what a far worse
outbreak would do.

## Argentina's timeline, March 2020

- **3 March:** the first confirmed case.
- **11 March:** the WHO declared a pandemic, with 34 cases confirmed in
  Argentina. Panic buying started that week (below).
- **12 March:** DNU 260/2020 widened the national health emergency: flights
  from affected zones suspended, isolation mandatory for certain cases.
- **Mid-March:** classes suspended and the borders closed. The sources read
  don't date these clearly; 15–16 March is from memory and **unconfirmed**.
- **20 March:** DNU 297/2020, the *Aislamiento Social, Preventivo y
  Obligatorio* (ASPO), announced the evening before: a national lockdown with
  fines, from 128 confirmed cases. Cinemas, theatres and shopping centres
  closed.
- So **17 days** from the first case to a national lockdown.
- Secondary, 2026-09-29:
  [es.wikipedia](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pandemia_de_COVID-19_en_Argentina),
  [El Cronista, chronology](https://www.cronista.com/economia-politica/cronologia-del-coronavirus-en-la-argentina-desde-el-paciente-cero-hasta-hoy/).
  The decrees themselves are on the
  [Boletín Oficial (DNU 297/2020)](https://www.boletinoficial.gob.ar/detalleAviso/primera/227042/20200320)

## What stayed open under the lockdown

- **DNU 297/2020, art. 6, exempted essential activities** from the ASPO,
  among them: wholesale and retail supermarkets and **neighbourhood shops
  (*comercios minoristas de proximidad*)**, **pharmacies**, hardware
  stores, vets, **garrafa supply**; the food, hygiene and medicine
  industries and their supply chains; maintenance of basic services
  (water, electricity, gas, communications) and emergency care; public
  transport, goods transport, oil, fuels and LPG. Everything else closed.
  Secondary, 2026-09-29:
  [Ministerio Público Fiscal, guide to the ASPO's exceptions](https://www.mpf.gob.ar/covid/files/2020/04/Actualizaci%C3%B3n-de-la-Gu%C3%ADa-de-Deberes-y-Excepciones-del-cumplimiento-del-ASPO-y-de-la-prohibici%C3%B3n-de-circular.pdf),

## Panic buying

- From the WHO's declaration, supermarkets and wholesalers saw "panic and
  paranoia buying": long queues and empty shelves. Chains asked customers to
  "self-ration" as hand sanitiser ran short.
- Major chains' March 2020 sales volume rose **23.3 %** on average, most in
  groceries and cleaning products.
- Secondary, 2026-09-29:
  [El Cronista, 2020-03-13](https://www.cronista.com/apertura-negocio/empresas/Coronavirus-supermercados-colapsados-por-temor-a-desabastecimiento-20200313-0003.html),

## Towns closing themselves off

- **Mayors in Buenos Aires province sealed their districts**, fearing the
  virus would get in and collapse their health systems. Ezeiza, Dolores,
  Olavarría, Almirante Brown, San Vicente and Maipú among others. Ezeiza's
  mayor was first, **blocking access roads with earth mounds**; entry was
  only for those who could prove residence or permitted work.
- The province first called isolating a municipality unconstitutional, then
  accepted closing some accesses, not all, "in the framework of the
  emergency", to control movement.
- **Zárate set up checkpoints at the city's accesses by 17 March 2020**,
  staffed by security forces and municipal staff, questioning whoever
  entered (one post named, at Lavalle and Teodoro Fels). In April, vehicles
  were disinfected and papers checked at the accesses.
- Secondary, 2026-09-29:

## Health systems at and past their limit

- **Argentina, second wave (April–May 2021).** ICU occupancy in the
  Buenos Aires metropolitan area (AMBA) reached 75 % officially, against
  under 70 % in the first wave. A survey by the Sociedad Argentina de
  Terapia Intensiva of 47 ICUs (1,139 beds) in the AMBA found **97 %**.
  Elective and scheduled surgery was cancelled to free ICU beds. Nationally,
  77.9 % in May. Secondary, 2026-09-29:
  [Infobae, 2021-05-03](https://www.infobae.com/salud/2021/05/03/segunda-ola-de-covid-19-las-razones-que-explican-por-que-la-ocupacion-de-camas-en-el-amba-esta-casi-colapsada/),
  [Consenso Salud](https://www.consensosalud.com.ar/record-de-ocupacion-en-las-unidades-de-terapia-intensiva-del-amba/),
  [Chequeado](https://chequeado.com/el-explicador/camas-de-terapia-intensiva-como-evoluciona-su-ocupacion-en-el-amba-y-en-el-pais/).
- **Guayaquil, Ecuador (late March–April 2020): collapse.** Hospitals
  closed their doors when beds ran out, and families went from one to the
  next. At the Hospital General Guasmo Sur the morgue filled and bodies
  piled up in corridors. Funeral services collapsed or refused to handle the
  dead; crematoria managed 24–25 a day. **Bodies were left in homes and on
  the streets**: police had a waiting list of 450 bodies to collect from
  homes, taking about four days per call. Deaths in Guayas province in the
  first half of April were about triple the usual, around 6,700.
  Secondary, 2026-09-29:
  [La Jornada, 2020-04-02](https://www.jornada.com.mx/2020/04/02/politica/002n1pol),
  [SinEmbargo, 2020-04-09](https://sinembargo.mx/09-04-2020/3764861),
  [Ámbito](https://www.ambito.com/mundo/guayaquil/la-crisis-del-coronavirus-no-da-tregua-amontonan-muertos-los-banos-hospitales-n5098325).
- **Bergamo, Italy (March 2020).** On 18 March army trucks (15 trucks,
  50 soldiers) carried coffins out of the city overnight to other provinces;
  the cemetery was overwhelmed and the crematorium, running nonstop, managed
  about 25 a day. The local paper's obituaries went from one page to ten.
  An ICU doctor: "every ventilator becomes like gold". Secondary,
  2026-09-29:
  [Newsweek](https://www.newsweek.com/coronavirus-italy-bergamo-army-bury-dead-1493194),
  [France 24, 2020-03-19](https://www.france24.com/en/20200319-coronavirus-death-toll-in-italy-overtakes-china-s-after-rising-to-3-405),
  [Snopes](https://www.snopes.com/fact-check/italy-army-coffins-coronavirus/).
- **Hospitals spread it.** In Italy health workers were about **10 %** of
  all cases by late March 2020; in Lombardy 13.8 % (3,957 of 28,750) by the
  third week of March. Outbreaks inside hospitals made them "a potential
  amplifier of the epidemic", inside the facility and outside it.
  Secondary, 2026-09-29:
  [Infection Control & Hospital Epidemiology](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/infection-control-and-hospital-epidemiology/article/regional-variation-in-trajectories-of-healthcare-worker-infections-during-the-covid19-pandemic-in-italy/2CB72BCD404C2B9533D0C5AAEEBB8AAB),
  [ICN](https://www.icn.ch/news/high-proportion-healthcare-workers-covid-19-italy-stark-warning-world-protecting-nurses-and).

## Essential services kept running

- **Atucha I, Atucha II and Embalse kept generating** from the start of the
  ASPO, with measures to protect staff; NA-SA reported a record year in 2020
  and about 11 % of the wholesale market in April. The specific staffing
  measures (isolated shifts, crews kept on site) weren't found. Secondary,
  2026-09-29:
  [NA-SA](https://www.na-sa.com.ar/es/prensa/nucleoel-ctrica-argentina-en-el-podio-de-las-generadoras-que-m-s-energ-a-aportaron-en-2020)
  (search summary).
