## Electricity

- **Henderson Municipal Power & Light (HMP&L)** is the city's utility. It owns
  transmission and distribution but no generation. Secondary, 2026-09-24:
  [S&P Global](https://www.spglobal.com/commodityinsights/en/market-insights/latest-news/coal/050118-big-rivers-says-to-stop-operating-henderson-kentucky-coal-plant-in-2019),
  [HMP&L](https://www.hmpl.com/).
- **Station One**, HMP&L's coal plant on Water Street by the river, ran
  **1956–2008**: 44 MW, three units, city-owned, closed by the end of 2008 for
  "rising costs and mounting environmental regulations". Confirmed,
  2026-09-25: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henderson_Station_One).
- **Station Two** (HMP&L Two) is part of **Sebree Station**, in the northeast
  corner of **Webster County**. It was retired in 2019, and its stacks and
  boiler were imploded in 2023.
  - Location: confirmed, 2026-09-25,
    [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sebree_Station).
  - Retirement and demolition: secondary, 2026-09-24,
    [S&P Global](https://www.spglobal.com/commodityinsights/en/market-insights/latest-news/coal/050118-big-rivers-says-to-stop-operating-henderson-kentucky-coal-plant-in-2019),
    [The Hendersonian](https://the-hendersonian.com/big-rivers-implodes-former-power-plant-boiler/).
- **HMP&L buys power through MISO**, the regional grid operator (Zone 6).
  Secondary, 2026-09-24:
  [APPA](https://www.publicpower.org/periodical/article/henderson-municipal-power-light-issues-rfo-30-mw-long-term-miso-zone-6).
- **Henderson County Solar**, 50 MW, sells all its output to HMP&L under a
  20-year contract signed in 2020, with operation planned for 2023. Secondary,
  2026-09-24: [Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20200730005282/en/Community-Energy-Announces-Agreement-With-Henderson-Municipal-Power-Light-HMPL-Enabling-50-Megawatts-of-New-Solar-Power-in-Western-Kentucky)
  (refuses automated readers),
  [KY PSC filing](https://psc.ky.gov/pscecf/2020-00391/kristen@gosssamfordlaw.com/02052021102318/Henderson_County_Solar_-_Notice_of_Filing_-_210205.pdf).
- **Grid-tied solar inverters shut down when the grid goes dead,** so a solar
  farm can't power a town on its own. Unconfirmed: general knowledge, no
  source read yet.
- **HMP&L substations reported:**
  - just east of the Borax Drive / US 41A-60 intersection;
  - just west of the Atkinson Street intersection;
  - one serving the north end of town and Highway 60 East.

  Secondary, 2026-09-24:
  [Tri-State Homepage](https://www.tristatehomepage.com/news/local-news/henderson-county/henderson-news/hmpl-crews-continuing-to-restore-power-to-customers-in-dark/)
  (refuses automated readers; read through a search summary).
- **The rural county: Kenergy.** Kenergy, a co-op headquartered in Henderson,
  is supplied by **Big Rivers Electric**. Secondary, 2026-09-24:
  [Big Rivers](https://www.bigrivers.com/member-cooperatives.html).
- **Robert D. Green Station**, part of Sebree Station: two units, **528 MW
  nameplate**, converted from coal to **natural gas in March 2022**.
  - Confirmed, 2026-09-25:
    [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_D._Green_Generating_Station).
  - Burns & McDonnell gives "500 MW" after conversion. Secondary:
    [Burns & McDonnell](https://www.burnsmcd.com/news/coal-to-gas-conversion-big-rivers-electric).
- **The Reid extra-high-voltage substation** sits on the Webster/Henderson
  county line. Secondary, 2026-09-24:
  [KY PSC](https://psc.ky.gov/agencies/psc/industry/electric/irp/201400166_122015.pdf).
- **Century Aluminum's Sebree smelter** draws about **368 MW** and needs
  385 MW firm. Secondary, 2026-09-24:
  [SEC 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000949157/000094915714000008/a12312013_q4x10-k.htm).

  [POWER](https://www.powermag.com/vectren-will-close-coal-units-add-renewables/).

## Water (Henderson Water Utility)

- **Distribution:** more than 220 miles of main, **12 storage tanks holding
  14.6 million gallons**, and **4 pumping stations**. The treatment plants'
  combined capacity is 16 million gallons a day. Confirmed, 2026-09-25:
  [HWU Operations](https://www.hkywater.org/222/Operations).
- **North Water Treatment Plant:**
  - averages **8.4 million gallons a day**, with a design capacity of 12;
  - serves the City of Henderson and the Henderson County Water District.

  Confirmed, 2026-09-25:
  [HWU Drinking Water](https://www.hkywater.org/197/Drinking-Water).
  - **Its raw water came from Station One's cooling-water discharge from 1961
    to 2008.** A direct Ohio River intake replaced it after the plant closed.
    The Water Street Treatment Plant was built in 1961, and its capacity
    doubled to 12 million gallons a day in 1990. Confirmed, 2026-09-25:
    [HWU Water Plant History](https://www.hkywater.org/200/Water-Plant-History).
- **South Water Treatment Plant:**
  - opened in 1996 and draws from the **Green River**;
  - averages 3.2 million gallons a day, with a design capacity of 4;
  - serves Sebree, Beech Grove, Robards and industrial customers, including
    Tyson Foods.

  Confirmed, 2026-09-25: HWU Drinking Water and Water Plant History, above.
- **Reservoirs:**
  - the original 3-million-gallon open-top concrete reservoir dates from
    1876;
  - the **Atkinson Park Tank**, a welded steel reservoir holding **4.5 million
    gallons**, replaced it in **1946** and is still in use;
  - in 1917 the utility installed an ultraviolet disinfection system, the
    first of its type.

  Confirmed, 2026-09-25: HWU Water Plant History, above.
- **What it means for the game (#260, #47).** Stored water is about 1.7 days
  of the North plant's normal average output. In an emptied town, demand
  drops far below that, so gravity storage would outlast the pumps. This is
  **derived**, not sourced: 14.6 ÷ 8.4.

## Gas and crossings

- **Texas Gas Transmission** runs pipelines through Henderson County, including
  the 23.5-mile Henderson Lateral to A.B. Brown. Secondary, 2026-09-24:
  [Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2022/08/31/2022-18790/texas-gas-transmission-llc-notice-of-availability-of-the-final-environmental-impact-statement-for).
- **The city's gas distributor was not confirmed.**
- **The US 41 Twin Bridges** cross the Ohio to Evansville, built in 1932 and
  1966, carrying about 40,000 vehicles a day. Secondary, 2026-09-24:
  [14 News](https://www.14news.com/story/6723321/twin-bridges-75-yrs-old-and-counting/).
