# How Argentines behaved in real crises — 2001, 2020, the floods

Real-world facts, not canon until a decision adopts them (`../Ashfall_Canon.md`
records what's decided). See `README.md` for the status labels. Gathered for
#492, at Tom's request (2026-10-06): the survivors the player meets are a mix,
person by person (#479), and that mix should rest on how people here have
actually behaved, for writing survivors (#480) and how they act (#12).

Documented, dated examples and the studies' broad findings, not a statistical
survey. Organisations and businesses are named only as sources; in the facts
they are described ("a supermarket chain's store").

## December 2001: the saqueos

- **Timing.** The looting began on 12 December, grew from the 16th and was
  general across the conurbano by the 18th–19th; the worst was in Greater
  Buenos Aires and Rosario, with episodes in most provinces. Counting
  requests for food at supermarkets as well as lootings, about **600 actions**
  in all. Deaths in the 19–20 December unrest are given as at least 32 (Anzorena)
  to 39, police repression included. Confirmed on the papers, 2026-10-06:
  [Nuñez 2021, *Aletheia* (UNLP)](https://doi.org/10.24215/18533701e104),
  citing Schuster et al. 2002 and Gordillo 2017;
  [Anzorena 2007, VII Jornadas de Sociología (UBA)](https://www.aacademica.org/000-106/109).
- **Asking came before taking.** Across the country, groups gathered in front
  of supermarkets asking for food, often women with children; many managers
  and owners handed out food, and the State sent food trucks, sometimes
  ending it, sometimes not. One owner gave out food to keep the crowd off his
  electrical goods, after a parish priest from a Church charity negotiated a
  truce. The requests were often taken for lootings, which fed the rumours.
  Confirmed on the paper (a day-by-day compilation from the national and
  provincial press), 2026-10-06:
  [Iñigo Carrera & Cotarelo, *La insurrección espontánea* (UNSAM)](https://cedinpe.unsam.edu.ar/sites/default/files/pdfs/inigo_carrera-cotarelo-2001.pdf).
- **What was looted.** Mid-sized supermarkets, butchers' and small meat
  shops, small Chinese-owned supermarkets, *almacenes*. The biggest chains'
  hypermarkets were mostly protected by police while smaller shops nearby
  were left in "liberated zones" (Auyero). Not only food was taken: wine and
  beer, appliances; in Campana, the staff's own clothes. Confirmed on Nuñez
  and Iñigo Carrera; Auyero's finding Secondary, from
  [La Nación, 2021-12-19](https://www.lanacion.com.ar/politica/saqueos-en-el-conurbano-el-fantasma-del-estallido-social-que-quedo-latente-y-revive-cada-diciembre-nid19122021/)
  and [a review of *La zona gris* (2007)](https://www.scielo.org.mx/scielo.php?script=sci_arttext&pid=S0187-57952008000200010).
- **Zárate–Campana.** On 19 December a supermarket chain's store in Campana
  was looted; the staff's clothes were taken, and the next morning looters came
  back for what was left and fought with the employees, mocking them for
  losing their jobs. "Rumours of looting spread across the whole north of the
  province." On the 21st there were still actions in Zárate–Campana. Confirmed
  on Iñigo Carrera & Cotarelo (citing the press), 2026-10-06. About 86
  detained in Zárate–Campana: Secondary, a search summary only, not read on
  its source. Nothing found about Lima itself.
- **Owners who fought, and owners who couldn't.** Shopkeepers lowered their
  shutters; some slept in their shops with guns. In Greater Buenos Aires,
  armed clashes between looters and shopkeepers caused **most of the deaths
  in the lootings**: on the evening of the 19th, two small-shop owners killed
  two looters, one at a Chinese-owned supermarket in Villa Fiorito; the owner
  was moved to another police station for fear of reprisal from the barrio.
  In one town an owner fired in the air, was stoned, and the shop was
  emptied. In Rosario, police, shop owners and neighbours exchanged fire.
  Confirmed on Iñigo Carrera & Cotarelo; the sleeping-with-guns quote
  Secondary,
  [swissinfo/EFE, 2021](https://www.swissinfo.ch/spa/relatos-salvajes-de-2001-saqueos-represi%C3%B3n-y-corralito-en-argentina/47202278).
- **Neighbours, kind.** A Chinese shopkeeper in Ciudadela was looted on
  20 December, mostly by people who came in a vehicle, and could only stand
  and cry. A neighbour took his family in for two days and residents helped
  clean up; the shop never reopened. Secondary, 2026-10-06:
  [El Cronista, 2021-12-21](https://www.cronista.com/informacion-gral/el-llanto-que-fue-icono-de-la-crisis-del-2001-donde-esta-hoy-el-almacenero-chino-al-que-le-saquearon-todo/).

### The night of the rumours

- **By day the rumour said "they're handing out food at the supermarket";
  by night it said "they're coming to loot our houses".** In two barrios of
  Hurlingham (Parque Quirno and San Jorge), the same neighbours who had gone
  to the supermarkets went home in the evening because word ran from mouth
  to mouth that people from other poor barrios (Fuerte Apache, Ciudadela,
  La Juanita and others named) were coming to loot homes. **Nobody came.**
  Confirmed on the paper, 2026-10-06:
  [Anzorena 2007](https://www.aacademica.org/000-106/109).
- **What they did:** locked doors that were normally left on the latch ("in
  the barrio you don't lock"), hid what they had, even the nappies taken that
  day, under beds and in wardrobes. Young people, men and the bolder women
  sat around bonfires on every corner with sticks, hammers, catapults,
  boiling water, piles of stones, machetes, knives, iron bars and a few
  guns. A shot in the air or a firecracker thrown on the fire was the alarm
  from one corner to the next. "A worker can't rob another worker." "It's
  war between the poor." Four years on they laughed at having believed it:
  "What were they going to loot from us?" Confirmed on Anzorena 2007.
- **It was everywhere.** The conurbano filled with residents' burning
  barricades, which slowed ambulances. In San Miguel de Tucumán, the
  government closed the courts and the legislature on a rumour that "hordes"
  were coming to free the detained; no column arrived. Confirmed on Iñigo
  Carrera & Cotarelo; the barricades Secondary,
  [radiográfica, 2021-12-19](https://radiografica.org.ar/2021/12/19/vivencias-del-19-y-20-de-diciembre-de-2001/).
- **Who started the rumours** is disputed: police and local political
  brokers (*punteros*) are named by Auyero and Ossona as spreading both kinds;
  Anzorena's interviewees deny it in their barrio. Secondary, La Nación
  2021-12-19 (above).
- **How it's remembered** (13 interviews in two barrios of San Francisco
  Solano, Quilmes, November 2015): the looter is always placed at a distance:
  "from over there", "the psychosis that the ones from Fuerte Apache were
  coming". A man in steady work denied there was any looting nearby ("here
  we're all workers"), though the avenue next to his barrio was looted. A
  woman remembered everyone indoors by eight at night, afraid of looters and
  police alike. Only a woman long unemployed and active in a community centre
  said it plainly: "they're the neighbours' kids", out of work and hungry.
  Confirmed on the paper, 2026-10-06: [Nuñez 2021](https://doi.org/10.24215/18533701e104).

## 2002: the cooperative side

- **Barter clubs (*clubes de trueque*).** The first met in Bernal, Quilmes, on
  1 May 1995 with 23 people. At the 2002 peak, figures run from about 2.5 to
  7 million participants and 4,500 to 8,000 clubs, depending on the source;
  IPS gives nearly 3 million. Members brought what they made or could do:
  tradespeople, doctors, dentists, psychologists, teachers. The barter credits
  collapsed during 2002 from forgery (by one account 90 % of the credits in
  circulation), credits issued without anything behind them, and prices
  rising up to 40-fold; by November 2002, about 250,000 active participants
  and 3,000 clubs. Secondary, 2026-10-06:
  [IPS, 2002-11-04](https://ipsnoticias.net/2002/11/argentina-auge-y-caida-del-trueque/),
  [IPS, 2009](https://ipsnoticias.net/2009/04/desarrollo-argentina-el-trueque-navega-contra-la-corriente/),
  search summaries of
  [Redalyc, "El trueque solidario"](https://www.redalyc.org/pdf/906/90611559002.pdf). The
  spread of figures is real; a single count wasn't found.
- **Neighbourhood assemblies (*asambleas barriales*).** About 30 in the
  capital by early January 2002; at the peak, August 2002, over 300 in the
  country and over 100 in the city of Buenos Aires. Meetings of 50–100 at
  first, settling to about 50 by March. Ages from teens to over 70:
  unemployed, retirees, shopkeepers, students. Besides politics they ran
  community kitchens, vegetable gardens, food fairs and festivals. An urban,
  mostly middle-class phenomenon of the capital and the big cities.
  Secondary, 2026-10-06:
  [Triguboff, *Revista del Centro Cultural de la Cooperación*](https://www.centrocultural.coop/revista/13/asambleas-populares-en-la-argentina-procesos-sociales-y-practicas-politicas-tras-la).
  Nothing found on assemblies in towns of Lima's size.
- **Community kitchens (*comedores*, *ollas populares*).** They multiplied in
  poor barrios in 2001–2002, run mostly by women, the same women often seen
  at the start of the lootings; in barrio memory the *olla* is the
  counterpart of the looting. No national count for 2002 was found. Secondary,
  search summaries, 2026-10-06:
  [Cuestiones de Sociología (UNLP)](https://www.memoria.fahce.unlp.edu.ar/art_revistas/pr.5898/pr.5898.pdf).
- **Hidden need.** "A lot of people were very bad off and tried not to show
  it outside their door. Whoever went out to ask did it secretly." Getting by
  meant community kitchens, neighbours passing on odd jobs, bingos among
  family and friends. Confirmed on [Nuñez 2021](https://doi.org/10.24215/18533701e104).

## 2020: the lockdown (ASPO)

### Kindness

- **Neighbours offered to shop for the old.** Notices in lifts offering to do
  the shopping for over-60s, the sick, the pregnant or anyone alone, from
  the first days of the lockdown. Secondary, 2026-10-06:
  [La Nación](https://www.lanacion.com.ar/lifestyle/te-doy-mano-la-mejor-version-cuarentena-nid2357815/),
  [La Nación](https://www.lanacion.com.ar/comunidad/cuarentena-desafios-jovenes-redes-ayudar-grupos-riesgo-nid2345342/)
  (search summaries).
- **Formal volunteering broke records.** The city of Buenos Aires' programme
  for older people had nearly 40,000 volunteers registered in its first month
  (shopping, pharmacy runs, walking dogs, phone calls), matched by
  proximity. A medical faculty's volunteer call drew about 7,000, five times
  the usual; an emergency-help NGO went from about 1,100 volunteers to over
  4,000. Secondary, 2026-10-06:
  [Infobae, 2020-08-31](https://www.infobae.com/tendencias/2020/08/31/solidaridad-en-tiempos-de-pandemia-se-registra-un-historico-record-de-nuevos-voluntarios-en-ong/).
- **Applause for health workers** from balconies at 21:00, one of the first
  rituals of the lockdown, called from 19 March. Secondary,
  [Diario de Cuyo, 2020-03-19](https://www.diariodecuyo.com.ar/enlasredes/Convocan-a-un-aplauso-masivo-en-apoyo-a-los-que-trabajan-para-combatir-el-coronavirus-20200319-0069.html).
- **Community kitchens grew.** Officially, people asking for food assistance
  in the country rose from 8 to 11 million in 2020; in the city of Buenos
  Aires, from 102,000 to over 353,000, and those eating at its 471 registered
  kitchens from 90,000 to 120,000. A survey of 49 city kitchens (May–
  September 2020): 79 % of the workers women; food from social
  organisations, donations (42 %) and the workers' own pockets (38 %), the
  State mentioned in 36 %. Confirmed on the paper (official figures as it
  quotes them), 2026-10-06:
  [Kasparian & Salgado 2021, XIV Jornadas de Sociología (UBA)](https://www.aacademica.org/000-074/139).
  One social organisation said its kitchens doubled from 1,000 to over 2,000,
  counting private houses that began to give out food. Secondary,
  [La Nación](https://www.lanacion.com.ar/politica/coronavirus-argentina-barrios-pie-instalo-2000-ollas-nid2355450/).

### Hostility

- **Notes against health workers where they lived.** April 2020: neighbours
  in buildings in Belgrano/Colegiales and Villa Ortúzar (Buenos Aires) posted
  signs telling doctors not to use the lift or move about the building; two
  doctors went to court. In the Libertad barrio of Merlo, a SAME ambulance
  doctor found a typed note on his door, wrapped in plastic and signed "the
  neighbours": "We know of your good work at the hospital and it's
  appreciated, but you must think of the neighbours. There are children and
  old people here." He: "I have nowhere else to go." An obstetrician was asked
  to stop seeing pregnant women. A nurse in Santa Fe: "We know you're a nurse,
  leave the barrio, you and your daughter, you'll infect us all." The
  national ombudsman opened a channel for such complaints on 9 April.
  Secondary, 2026-10-06:
  [La Nación](https://www.lanacion.com.ar/sociedad/coronavirus-argentina-denunciaron-discriminacion-vecinos-belgrano-intimaron-nid2350110/),
  [Diario de Cuyo, 2020-04-17](https://www.diariodecuyo.com.ar/argentina/Es-medico-y-los-vecinos-le-pidieron-que-se-mudara-No-tengo-otro-lugar-para-irme-20200417-0045.html),
  [Infobae, 2020-04-09](https://www.infobae.com/coronavirus/2020/04/09/el-defensor-del-pueblo-recibira-denuncias-por-discriminacion-de-vecinos-hacia-el-personal-de-salud-que-trabaja-contra-el-covid-19/).
- **The suspected case.** Córdoba, 25 March–1 April 2020: a foreign-born
  technician waiting on a test. A neighbourhood association's president sent
  his address round by WhatsApp; there were calls to paint his brother's
  restaurant and break its windows, and a phone call: "Don't go to sleep,
  tonight we're setting you on fire." Police guarded the house at night. The
  test was negative. "I was more afraid of my neighbours than of the
  coronavirus." Secondary, 2026-10-06:
  [Infobae, 2020-04-03](https://www.infobae.com/coronavirus/2020/04/03/esperaba-los-resultados-del-test-y-vivio-cinco-dias-de-terror-le-tuve-mas-miedo-a-mis-vecinos-que-al-coronavirus/).
  More than half the complaints to the national anti-discrimination
  institute after 20 March were about such behaviour. Secondary, search
  summary of
  [Infobae, 2020-04-02](https://www.infobae.com/coronavirus/2020/04/02/crecen-los-casos-de-discriminacion-y-xenofobia-durante-la-cuarentena-obligatoria/).
- **Reporting neighbours.** A free national line, 134, for reporting people
  breaking isolation, opened in mid-March (Infobae's report of its first day
  is dated the 18th): about 2,400 calls the first day
  (130 cases investigated), about 9,000 by the start of the ASPO on the 20th,
  and nearly 40,000 within weeks, of which 2,843 went on for evaluation.
  Secondary, 2026-10-06:
  [Infobae, 2020-03-18](https://www.infobae.com/sociedad/policiales/2020/03/18/en-su-primer-dia-la-linea-134-para-denunciar-a-personas-que-violan-la-cuarentena-recibio-2400-llamados-y-se-investigan-130-nuevos-casos/),
  search summaries of the others. The exact date of the 40,000 wasn't read.
- **A barrio sealed.** Villa Azul (Quilmes–Avellaneda) was cordoned off by
  police in late May 2020 with 53 cases; residents organised in assemblies
  to demand food, and donations from people and clubs were at first stopped
  at the cordon. Secondary, 2026-10-06:
  [Infobae, 2020-05-25](https://www.infobae.com/sociedad/2020/05/25/la-villa-azul-desde-adentro-un-barrio-cercado-y-una-cancha-de-futbol-como-posible-foco-de-los-53-contagios-de-coronavirus/).

## Small towns

No study comparing small towns with the cities was found. The examples:

- **Residents closing their own towns.** March 2020, La Pampa: earth mounds
  on the access roads of several small towns (Victorica, Lonquimay, Rancul,
  Luan Toro, Macachín); a driver crashed into one at Luan Toro. In Córdoba,
  the courts warned municipalities that closing a town's accesses was a
  crime. In Tilisarao (San Luis), September 2020, residents fed up with being
  shut in removed the mounds themselves with shovels. Secondary, 2026-10-06:
  [En Boca de Todos, 2020-03-23](https://www.enbocadetodoshd.com.ar/provinciales/2020/3/23/chocaron-contra-un-monticulo-de-tierra-que-bloquea-luan-toro-por-la-cuarentena-40851.html),
  [La Voz de San Justo](http://www.lavozdesanjusto.com.ar/noticias/articulo/la-justicia-de-cordoba-advirtio-a-los-municipios-que-es-delito-penal-cerrar-accesos-a-los-poblados-84080),
  [Cadena 3](https://www.cadena3.com/noticia/juntos/rebelion-en-san-luis-quitaron-los-terraplenes-en-un-pueblo_270989)
  (search summaries).
- **Against one family from outside.** Quirós (Catamarca), 13–14 June 2020:
  about 200 residents cut the national route for three and a half hours
  because a family from Buenos Aires province, related to a former
  councillor, had been let in and went shopping the day it arrived. They
  agreed with the Gendarmería that no one from another province would enter.
  Secondary, 2026-10-06:
  [El Esquiú, 2020-06-14](https://www.elesquiu.com/sociedad/2020/6/14/otra-pueblada-por-el-ingreso-de-portenos-352715.html).
- **The first case in a town of about 10,000.** Suardi (Santa Fe), 16
  September 2020, after six months with none: the woman's name went round at
  once and she and her husband spoke on the local radio; over 150 people went
  into isolation as contacts. Rumours blamed local politicians' trips;
  neighbouring towns treated Suardi's people "like lepers". And a young man
  posted "I'll run errands for free" and six others joined him within hours.
  "On a small scale, solidarity, fear and confusion." Secondary (a local
  writer's account), 2026-10-06:
  [Dejame que lo piense, 2020-10-16](https://dejamelopensar.com.ar/2020/10/16/el-virus-en-pueblo-chico/).
  In Cutral Co (Neuquén), the first case's family complained of being shamed
  and judged by neighbours. Secondary, search summary only.
- **Everyone knows everyone** cuts both ways in these accounts: the help is
  personal and fast, and so is the naming. That is this session's reading
  of a handful of cases, not a finding. **Unconfirmed**; a study of stigma
  or mutual aid in small Argentine towns would confirm or refute it.

## Cut off: floods

- **La Plata, 2 April 2013.** Up to about 400 mm of rain in four hours; 89
  deaths confirmed by the courts, about 70 % over 65; some 190,000 affected
  and 120,000 electricity users cut off. No warning and no evacuation plan.
  Neighbours did most of the rescuing that night, with boats, trucks, ropes
  and inflatable mattresses, and less-flooded houses took in the rest; in the
  Ringuelet settlement, water stood 17 hours in the houses. Donations from
  across the country overwhelmed the collection points. Secondary,
  2026-10-06:
  [es.wikipedia](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inundaci%C3%B3n_en_La_Plata_de_2013);
  Confirmed on the paper:
  [Aversa, Rotger & Senise 2020, *Bitácora Urbano Territorial*](https://www.redalyc.org/journal/748/74864040017/html/);
  [Diagonales](https://www.diagonales.com/municipio/rescate-en-oscuridad--con-el-agua-al-pecho--una-soga-y-un-colchon-inflable-como-unicos-recursos--memorias-platenses-de-la-inundacion_a6428cc952f1e3da87901f22a)
  (search summary).
- **And theft.** A woman who went to an evacuation centre in 2013 came back
  to find her electric oven, iron, washing machine and speaker gone. In the
  2023 flood she stayed: "If you leave your house, they rob you." Secondary,
  2026-10-06:
  [La Nación, 2023-08-18](https://www.lanacion.com.ar/sociedad/si-te-vas-de-tu-casa-te-roban-el-temor-que-dejo-la-inundacion-en-la-plata-nid18082023/).
- **Santa Fe, 29 April 2003.** The Salado came through an unfinished
  defence and covered a third of the city: over 150,000 people fled within
  hours, 27 died from direct causes. Those who could piled belongings on
  their roofs and **stayed on the roofs, keeping watch together against
  thieves and looters**, until Army boats took them off or brought the day's
  food. Secondary, 2026-10-06:
  [El Litoral](https://www.ellitoral.com/especiales/inundacion-2003-santafe),
  [Radio Rafaela](https://radiorafaela.com.ar/regionales/a-21-anios-de-la-tragica-inundacion-de-santa-fe-cuando-el-rio-salado-tapo-un-tercio-de-la-ciudad)
  (search summaries).

## What the sources share

The studies' own conclusions, then this session's reading.

- **Rumour does more than looters.** In 2001 the rumour started the lootings
  and ended them, and the night's fear was of an enemy who never came
  (Anzorena; Iñigo Carrera & Cotarelo; Schuster et al. via Nuñez). Confirmed
  as the studies' finding.
- **The threat is always "from over there".** People remember the looter as
  someone from another barrio; those closest to need say "the neighbours'
  kids" (Nuñez). Confirmed as the study's finding.
- **The same people, both ways.** The women at the supermarket doors in the
  morning ran the *ollas* afterwards; the neighbours who went to loot the
  supermarket guarded their own corner that night. Read in Anzorena and the
  kitchens literature; the generalisation is this session's.
- **Help goes to known people, fear to unknown ones**: the old neighbour on
  the landing, the doctor named on a note, the family from Buenos Aires, the
  people from Fuerte Apache. **Unconfirmed**, this session's reading across
  the cases above, not a study's finding.
