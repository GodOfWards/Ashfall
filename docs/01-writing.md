# 01 — Writing

How the game's text reads, and what it may name. The home of the tone, the
real-place rules, and language and naming. Read by a planning session when
the discussion reaches game text; a handoff quotes whatever a coding session
needs from here.

The examples below are illustrative, written for this guide. They are not
game text: a guide that quotes the game goes stale the moment the game is
rewritten.

## The spirit

Ashfall is a quiet-apocalypse survival game. The collapse has already
happened; the player wakes into its aftermath, not its beginning. Nothing
explains what happened. The world explains itself through what people left
behind.

## Tone

- **Understatement over drama.** The horror is in the mundane detail, not in
  adjectives. *A mug on the draining board, the tea bag still in it*, not
  *the stench of decay fills the kitchen*.
- **Second person, present tense, plain sentences.** No purple prose, no
  narrator commenting on events. The player observes; the text doesn't
  editorialise.
- **Absence as storytelling.** A place's story is told through what its
  occupants didn't finish doing: a half-packed bag by the door, a calendar
  nobody turned. Never through exposition.
- **Ordinary domestic detail is the texture of the world.** It should feel
  like a real, specific small town that stopped, not a generic apocalypse
  backdrop.
- **The player's own life is specific.** Other homes can be described against
  the player's own (*the same layout as yours, tidier*), which makes the
  player a person with a place, not a spawn point.

## Checklist for a new description

A room, item or event description:

- Would read the same if a person, not a game, wrote it about a real place
  they walked into. If it sounds like copy, rewrite it.
- Shows rather than states: a concrete object over a mood word ("dusty",
  "eerie", "abandoned").
- Runs one to three sentences. Longer risks becoming exposition.
- Never explains the collapse. A place implies its own small piece of it;
  no description tries to explain the whole event.

Functional UI text (button labels, exit labels, status readouts) is held to
clarity, not to this tone. A plain orientation label is correct as it is,
not a missed chance for atmosphere.

## A real place

The town is Lima, in the Partido de Zárate, Buenos Aires province, Argentina,
and the world is as real and accurate as it can be made. Streets, buildings,
utilities and infrastructure carry their real names, and what the game says
about them (where they are, what they do, how they connect) matches the real
town.

A fact is checked against a source when it is written, never recalled or
invented, and recorded in `docs/canon/reference/` with its source. That
folder's `README.md` is the home of how facts are looked up, researched and
recorded, and of what to do when one isn't there. A fact that can't be
confirmed is marked unconfirmed, never filled with a plausible one. Adding
something the real town lacks is a design decision, and Tom's.

A real name doesn't license exposition. A real power plant is still described
through what its last crew left behind.

## Brands and business names

- **No real product brand appears.** An item is "a sachet of milk" or "a bag
  of yerba", never its maker's name.
- **A private business is named by what it is:** "the pharmacy", "a bank",
  "the petrol station", "the hardware store". It still stands at its real
  site.
- **An institution with a proper name keeps it:** Correo Argentino, Banco
  Nación, the church of San Isidro Labrador.
- **A government building is named by what it is:** "the police station",
  "the municipal hospital", "the primary school".
- Fictional brands and trading names are lore, for later.

## Language

The game is written in English.

- **Streets, districts and towns keep their Spanish names**, spelled as they
  are locally, accents included, never translated or anglicised: "Calle 15",
  "Barrio Atucha", "Zárate".
- **Everything else is named in English.** A place by what it is, even where
  its real name is Spanish ("Lima station", "the riverside walk"), and an item
  too ("a pack of biscuits").
- **The exception is a word with no English equivalent:** *yerba mate*,
  *mate*, *dulce de leche*, *alfajores*.

The reference files record every real name; the game text uses the English
one.
